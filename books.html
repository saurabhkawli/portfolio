The Reading Room

Every book is a door. Mythology and mystery are the two I keep walking through.

Chapter the First

It was a dark and quiet evening, the kind made for turning pages. The tea had gone cold. Nobody minded.

Somewhere between the third chapter and the fourth, the detective noticed what everyone else had missed.

I love reading books. Give me a myth older than memory or a mystery with a locked door, and the rest of the world can wait.

The best stories never really end — they just move in and stay with you.

II · The two that made me

A first love, and a forever one.

My first book

Robinson Crusoe

Daniel Defoe · 1719

The first book I ever finished. One man, one island and twenty-eight years of making do with whatever washed ashore. Looking back, maybe that's where it all started — the adventures, and the habit of solving problems with whatever you've got.

“Thus fear of danger is ten thousand times more terrifying than danger itself.”
All-time favourite

A Little Princess

Frances Hodgson Burnett · 1905

Sara Crewe loses everything — her father, her fortune, her room — and still chooses kindness. It's the book that taught me that dignity isn't something the world hands you. You carry it inside.

“If I am a princess in rags and tatters, I can be a princess inside.”

IV · Tales of Mahadev

The stories I always return to.

I've always loved Hindu mythology, and the stories of Mahadev most of all. The destroyer who is also the most compassionate. The ascetic on Kailash who is also the perfect householder. Every story holds a lesson that still works today.

नीलकण्ठ

Neelkanth

When the gods and the asuras churned the cosmic ocean, the first thing to rise was Halahala — a poison strong enough to end the worlds. Mahadev drank it and held it in his throat, which turned blue. Compassion, paid for by himself.

गङ्गाधर

Gangadhar

When the mighty Ganga descended from the heavens, her force could have shattered the earth. Shiva caught her in his matted locks and released her gently — strength used to soften a blow, not to strike one.

नटराज

Nataraja

The Lord of Dance. His Tandava is the rhythm of the cosmos — creation, preservation and destruction in one endless dance. Endings, it says, are just the beat before a new beginning.

अर्धनारीश्वर

Ardhanarishvara

Half Shiva, half Parvati, in one form. The reminder that the universe runs on balance — strength and softness, stillness and motion.

ज्योतिर्लिङ्ग

Jyotirlinga

Brahma and Vishnu once argued over who was greater. A pillar of infinite light split the sky; Brahma flew up and Vishnu dug down, and neither ever found its end. Of the twelve Jyotirlingas, I've stood before three so far — Somnath, Bhimashankar and Trimbakeshwar.
